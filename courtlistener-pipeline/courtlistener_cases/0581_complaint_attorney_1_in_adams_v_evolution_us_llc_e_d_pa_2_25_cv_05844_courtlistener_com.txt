II. Operative Facts.

10. On or about May 20, 2024, Defendant hired Plaintiff Long as a game presenter.

11. Onor about June 3, 2024, Defendant hired Plaintiff Adams as a game presenter.

12. At the time of her hire, Plaintiff Adams was approximately three (3) months
pregnant, and she disclosed her pregnancy to Defendant during her interview, and again
subsequent to her hire.

13. At the time of her hire, Plaintiff Adams’ shift was 11pm to 7am, which Plaintiff

Adams was finding difficult to work due to her pregnancy.

14, On or about July 14", Plaintiff Adams asked her overnight manager, Ernie, to be
switched to an earlier shift.

15. Around this time, Plaintiff Long also discovered that she was pregnant.

16. Plaintiff Long felt very ill as a result of her pregnancy and began to miss days at
work and/or have to leave her shift early.

17. _—- Around the end of July of 2024, Plaintiff Long informed Defendant of her
pregnancy, and requested to be moved to a different shift.

18. It usually took Defendant approximately two (2) weeks to move Plaintiffs’ shifts.

19. During the interim, Plaintiff Adams did not work, and accrued attendance
occurrences as a result.

20. Plaintiff Long attempted to continue to work her original shift until the shift
change took place, but was still having difficulty due to morning sickness and was also accruing
attendance occurrences.

21. When Plaintiff Adams returned to work her new shift, Defendant told Plaintiff
Adams she was essentially on a “final warning” due to her attendance, and was told she could
not miss any more work.

22. Plaintiff Adams was even obliged to come to work with Covid, and Defendant
only allowed her to leave after her co-workers complained.

23. On or about August 19, 2024, Plaintiff Adams went to the ER as she felt
extremely sick due to her pregnancy.

24. Plaintiff Adams was admitted to the hospital overnight, and she contacted

Defendant to advise them of the situation, and that she would be unable to come to work.

26. Defendant refused to accept any doctor’s notes from Plaintiff Adams to cover any
absences.

27. — Plaintiff Adams was not offered any accommodation paperwork to cover any
absences due to her pregnancy.

28. During this time, Plaintiff Long had also returned to work on her new shift and
her attendance improved.

29. However, in mid-August of 2024, Plaintiff Long was contacted by Defendant’s
HR representative, Brook, who indicated that Plaintiff Long should have been provided
accommodation paperwork to be completed when she first informed Defendant of her pregnancy
and requested a shift change.

30. Brook indicated that this paperwork would have covered Plaintiff Long’s
absences due to her pregnancy.

31. Brook advised Plaintiff Long to try and get the paperwork back to her within a
week or two.

2024, and would have the paperwork completed and returned at that time,

IMPORTANT TIME LIMITS — 90 DAYS TO FILE A LAWSUIT

If you choose to file a lawsuit against the respondent(s) named in the charge of discrimination,
you must file a complaint in court within 90 days of the date you receive EEOC’s official
notice of dismissal. You should keep a record of the date you received EEOC’s official notice
of dismissal. Once this 90-day period has passed, your right to sue based on the charge referred
to in this Notice will be lost. If you intend to consult an attorney, you should do so promptly.
Give your attorney a copy of this Notice, and the record of your receiving EEOC’s official notice
of dismissal (email or envelope).

If your lawsuit includes a claim under the Equal Pay Act (EPA), you must file your complaint in
court within 2 years (3 years for willful violations) of the date you did not receive equal pay. This
time limit for filing an EPA lawsuit is separate from the 90-day filing period under Title VII, the
ADA, GINA, the ADEA, or the PWFA referred to above. Therefore, if you also plan to sue under
Title VII, the ADA, GINA, the ADEA, or the PWFA, in addition to suing on the EPA claim, your
lawsuit must be filed within 90 days of your receipt of EEOC’s official notice of dismissal and
within the 2- or 3-year EPA period.

Your lawsuit may be filed in U.S. District Court or a State court of competent jurisdiction.
Whether you file in Federal or State court is a matter for you to decide after talking to your
attorney. You must file a “complaint” that contains a short statement of the facts of your case
which shows that you are entitled to relief. Filing this Notice is not enough. For more information
about filing a lawsuit, go to https://www.eeoc.gov/employees/lawsuit.cfn.
