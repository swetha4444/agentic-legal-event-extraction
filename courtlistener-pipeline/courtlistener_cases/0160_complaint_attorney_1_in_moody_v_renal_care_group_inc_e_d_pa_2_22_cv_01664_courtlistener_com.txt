                                      CIVIL ACTION COMPLAINT

        Plaintiff, Lashonda Moody (hereinafter referred to as “Plaintiff”), by and through her

undersigned counsel, hereby avers as follows:

                                                I. Introduction

        1.       Plaintiff has initiated this action to redress violations by Defendant of Title VII of

the Civil Rights Act of 1964 ("Title VII" - 42 U.S.C. §§ 2000e, et. seq.) and the Pennsylvania

Human Relations Act ("PHRA - 43 Pa. C.S. §§ 951 et. seq.).1 Plaintiff asserts herein that she

was unlawfully terminated from her employment with Defendant in violation of these laws and

seeks damages as set forth more fully herein.

                                 IV.     Factual Background

        9.    The foregoing paragraphs are incorporated herein in their entirety as if set forth in

full.

        10.   Plaintiff was hired by Defendant on or about April 19, 2021 as a Patient Care

Technician.

        11.   Plaintiff remained employed with Defendant until on or about June 19, 2021,

when she was unlawfully terminated from her job.

        12.   During her employment with Defendant, Plaintiff was supervised by Kathy

Cooney, Facility Administrator (hereinafter “Cooney”).

        13.   Plaintiff is a practicing Muslim.

        14.   After becoming employed with Defendant, Plaintiff requested the reasonable

accommodation to pray while at work.

        15.   While Plaintiff was initially granted her accommodation to pray at work, it clearly

became an issue with her trainer and Defendant’s management, including but not limited to

Cooney.

        16.   In fact, shortly following her hire, Plaintiff began being treated with hostility and

animosity after Defendant’s management became aware of her religious beliefs and need for

accommodations.

        17.   By way of specific example (but not intending to be the only one), Plaintiff

requested to leave work for a brief period of time so that she could pray with other members of

her religion one day during the Ramadan holiday, and she was given permission to do so by

Cooney.




                                                  3
         Case 2:22-cv-01664-NIQA Document 1 Filed 04/29/22 Page 4 of 10




       18.     However, when Plaintiff returned to work after engaging in religious prayer one

day during the Ramadan holiday (as discussed in Paragraph 17 of the instant Civil Action

Complaint), Plaintiff was informed that she needed to take the rest of the day off because she left

work to pray (even though she had sought and was given permission to leave).

       19.     By way of further example, Plaintiff would pray at work almost every single day,

which was in no way a disruption to Defendant’s daily business because she would do so outside

of the presence of patients, and it would be extremely brief. However, one day, Plaintiff was

called into Cooney’s office and asked questions about when and how often she prayed during the

day and then stated that she “prayed too much.”

       20.     In addition to the overt acts of discriminatory treatment based on her religion and

requests for religious accommodations, Plaintiff was also treated in a rude and condescending

manner, denied proper training, sent home early several times, and had her concerns regarding

Defendant’s management’s treatment of patients and violations of policies ignored.

       21.     Plaintiff was also pretextually reprimanded following her requests for reasonable

religious accommodations, including but not limited to being reprimanded for lateness. However,

(1) Plaintiff’s trainer (who also seemingly took issue with Plaintiff’s need to pray at work)

instructed Plaintiff to arrive after the start of her shift, and then Plaintiff was disciplined for

doing so; and (2) Plaintiff had difficulties clocking in, which Defendant’s management and IT

department were made aware of.

       22.     The aforesaid discriminatory and retaliatory harassment became so severe that

Plaintiff became hesitant to pray at work and even decreased the number of times she would do

so to avoid hostility and animosity from Defendant’s management.

          23.    After being subjected to constant discrimination and retaliation for approximately

two months, Plaintiff was ultimately terminated from her employment for pretextual reasons.

                                        First Cause of Action
                Violations of Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 ("Title VII")
          ([1] Religious Discrimination; [2] Retaliation; [3] Hostile Work Environment)

          24.    The foregoing paragraphs are incorporated herein in their entirety as if set forth in

full.

          25.    Plaintiff was Plaintiff was subjected to a hostile work environment through

disparate treatment, pretextual admonishment, discriminatory comments and/or demeaning

treatment during her employment with Defendant because of her religion and/or requests for

religious accommodations.

          26.    Plaintiff was also: (1) denied a reasonable religious accommodation; and (2)

terminated due to her religious beliefs and/or her requested religious accommodations.

          27.    Plaintiff properly exhausted her administrative remedies by timely filing a Charge

with the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission ("EEOC") asserting these claims and by

filing the instant lawsuit within 90 days of receiving a notice of case closure and/or a right-to-sue

letter.

          28.    Defendant’s actions as aforesaid constitute violations of Title VII.

          WHEREFORE, Plaintiff prays that this Court enter an order providing that:

          A.     Defendant is to be prohibited from continuing to maintain its illegal policy,

practice, or custom of discriminating and retaliating against employees and are to be ordered to

promulgate an effective policy against such discrimination/retaliation and to adhere thereto;

          B.     Defendant is to compensate Plaintiff, reimburse Plaintiff, and make Plaintiff

whole for any and all pay and benefits Plaintiff would have received had it not been for

Defendant’s illegal actions, including but not limited to back pay, front pay, bonuses and medical

and other benefits. Plaintiff should be accorded those benefits illegally withheld from the date he

first suffered discrimination/retaliation at the hands of Defendant until the date of verdict;

       C.      Plaintiff is to be awarded punitive damages as permitted by applicable law in an

amount believed by the Court or trier of fact to be appropriate to punish Defendant for its willful,

deliberate, malicious and outrageous conduct, and to deter Defendant from engaging in such

misconduct in the future;

       D.      Plaintiff is to be accorded any and all other equitable and legal relief as the Court

deems just, proper, and appropriate (including but not limited to emotional distress/pain and

suffering damages - where permitted under applicable law(s)).

       E.      Plaintiff is to be awarded the costs and expenses of this action and reasonable

legal fees as provided by applicable federal and state law;

       F.      Any verdict in favor of Plaintiff is to be molded by the Court to maximize the

financial recovery available to Plaintiff in light of the caps on certain damages set forth in

applicable federal law;

       G.      Plaintiff’s claims are to receive a trial by jury to the extent allowed by applicable

law. Plaintiff has also endorsed this demand on the caption of this Complaint in accordance with

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 38(b).
