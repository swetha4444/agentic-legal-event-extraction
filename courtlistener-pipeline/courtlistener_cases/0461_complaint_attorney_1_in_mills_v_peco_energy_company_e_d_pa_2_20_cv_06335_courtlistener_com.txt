                                CIVIL ACTION COMPLAINT

I. Parties and Reasons for Jurisdiction.

       1.       Plaintiff, JASON MILLS (hereinafter “Plaintiff”) is an adult individual residing at

the above address.

       2.       Defendant, PECO ENERGY COMPANY (“PECO”) is a business corporation

organized by and operating under to the laws of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania owning,

operating and doing business at the above captioned address.

       3.       Defendant, EXELON (“Exelon”) is a business corporation organized by and

operating under to the laws of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania owning, operating and doing

business at the above captioned address. Both Defendants are hereinafter referred to as

“Defendants”.
            Case 2:20-cv-06335-RBS Document 1 Filed 12/16/20 Page 5 of 15




       4.       At all times material hereto, Defendants qualify as Plaintiff’s employer pursuant

to the Title VII of the Civil Rights Act and the Pennsylvania Human Relations Act (“PHRA”).

       5.       Plaintiff has exhausted his administrative remedies pursuant to the Equal

Employment Opportunity Act and the Pennsylvania Human Relations Act. (See Exhibit A, a

true and correct copy of a “right-to-sue” letter issued by the Equal Employment Opportunity

Commission.)

       6.       This action is instituted pursuant to the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the

Pennsylvania Human Relations Act and applicable federal law.

       7.       Jurisdiction is conferred by 28 U.S.C. §§ 1331 and 1343.

       8.       Supplemental jurisdiction over the Plaintiff’s state law claims is conferred

pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1367.

       9.       Pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1391(b)(1) and (b)(2), venue is properly laid in this

district because Defendants conduct business in this district, and because a substantial part of the

acts and/or omissions giving rise to the claims set forth herein occurred in this judicial district.

Plaintiff was working in the Eastern District of Pennsylvania at the time of the illegal actions by

Defendants as set forth herein.

II. Operative Facts.

       10.      On or about April 30, 2018, Defendants hired Plaintiff as a supervisor of testing.

       11.      Plaintiff, an African-American man, was the only African-American in a

management position in Defendants’ Berwyn location, out of approximately twenty (20)

leadership roles.

       12.      During his employment, Plaintiff had no issues with his work performance, no

disciplinary actions, and all of his reviews were positive.
           Case 2:20-cv-06335-RBS Document 1 Filed 12/16/20 Page 6 of 15




       13.     On November 18, 2019, Plaintiff arrived at work in his company-issued vehicle,

shut the door, and discovered the back window of the vehicle was broken.

       14.     Plaintiff believed the initial damage happened while his vehicle was parked on his

street at home, with the window fully shattering when he shut the door while exiting the vehicle

at work.

       15.     Plaintiff reported the incident to Defendants’ manager, Roy, and contacted a

repair shop to coordinate the repair of the window.

       16.     Plaintiff then submitted an incident report to Defendants stating he believed the

vehicle had been vandalized while parked on his street at home; Roy approved of writing the

condition report.

       17.     Plaintiff was under this belief because his own personal vehicle sustained similar

damage while parked on his street a few months prior.

       18.     On January 6, 2020, almost two (2) months following the incident, Plaintiff was

summoned to a security investigation.

       19.     During this investigation, Plaintiff was questioned for over two (2) hours about

the damage to the vehicle.

       20.     Plaintiff felt he was being forced into falsely admitting that he was covering up

what “actually” happened to the vehicle, as Defendants seemed to believe that Plaintiff had

thrown one of his crutches through the window and broke it, an incident in which he believed

never occurred.

       21.     Plaintiff maintained that while he did not know how the window had gotten

broken, it was his belief that someone on his street at home was vandalizing vehicles as his own

personal vehicle had sustained similar damage.
         Case 2:20-cv-06335-RBS Document 1 Filed 12/16/20 Page 7 of 15




       22.     Defendants refused to accept this proof and told Plaintiff “it was irrelevant”.

       23.     Plaintiff also became aware that Defendants subjected his girlfriend, also an

employee for Defendants, to questioning about Plaintiff’s character and “what really happened.”

       24.     Plaintiff was suspended with pay following this questioning while the

investigation continued.

       25.     The following Friday, Plaintiff was again summoned for a second round of

questioning, during which Defendants’ security representatives told Plaintiff that his “story

didn’t work for them.”

       26.     On February 19, 2020, Plaintiff received a termination letter dated February 13,

2020, stating his employment was being terminated for “violation of the Exelon Code of

Business Conduct.”

       27.     Prior to this incident, Plaintiff had complained about several instances of

harassment relating to other employees of Defendants’ management team.

       28.     In one such instance, Defendants’ prior manager William Spross, a Caucasian

made, made derogatory statements about female employees, and Defendants’ foreman John

Dohery, also a Caucasian man, threatened jobs and “punished” employees by assigning them

jobs that were further away or refused to engage with them if they had questions.

PRIVATE SUIT RIGHTS               --   Equal Pay Act (EPA):

EPA suits must be filed in court within 2 years (3 years for willful violations) of the alleged EPA underpayment: back
pay due for violations that occurred more than 2 years (3 years) before you file suit may not be collectible. For
example, if you were underpaid under the EPA for work performed from 7/1/08 to 12/1/08, you should file suit
before 7/1/10 – not 12/1/10 -- in order to recover unpaid wages due for July 2008. This time limit for filing an EPA
suit is separate from the 90-day filing period under Title VII, the ADA, GINA or the ADEA referred to above.
Therefore, if you also plan to sue under Title VII, the ADA, GINA or the ADEA, in addition to suing on the EPA
claim, suit must be filed within 90 days of this Notice and within the 2- or 3-year EPA back pay recovery period.
