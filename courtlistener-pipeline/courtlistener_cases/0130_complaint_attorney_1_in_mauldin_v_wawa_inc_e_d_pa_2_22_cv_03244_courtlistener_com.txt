                                  CIVIL ACTION COMPLAINT

I. Parties and Reasons for Jurisdiction.

       1.      Plaintiff, ANTOINETTE MAULDIN (hereinafter “Plaintiff”) is an adult

individual residing at the above address.

       2.      Defendant, WAWA, INC. is a business corporation organized by and operating

under the laws of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania, and having a principal place of business

at the above captioned address.

       3.      Defendant, WAWA, INC. is a business corporation organized by and operating

under the laws of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania and owning, operating and doing business

at the above captioned address. Both Defendants are hereinafter referred to as “Defendants.”

       4.      At all times material hereto, Defendants, acting together, in concert, by and on

each other’s behalf. At all times material hereto, Defendants were Plaintiff’s employer(s).

       5.      Plaintiff has exhausted her administrative remedies pursuant to the Equal

Employment Opportunity Act and the Pennsylvania Human Relations Act. (See Exhibit A, true

and correct copies of two “right-to-sue” letters issued by the Equal Employment Opportunity

Commission and Pennsylvania Human Relations Commission).

       6.      This action is instituted for violations of Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964

(42 U.S.C. §2000 et seq.) Plaintiff was constructively terminated, subjected to a sexually hostile

work environment and subjected to retaliation after complaining of sexual harassment.

       7.      Jurisdiction is conferred by 28 U.S.C. §1331 and §1343.

       8.      Supplemental jurisdiction over the Plaintiff’s state law claims is conferred

pursuant to 28 U.S.C. §1367.

       9.      Pursuant to 28 U.S.C. §1391(b)(1) and (b)(2), venue is properly laid in this

district, because Defendants conduct business in this district, and because a substantial part of the

acts, conduct and/or omissions giving rise to the claim set forth herein occurred in this judicial

district. Plaintiff was working in the Eastern District of Pennsylvania at the time of the illegal

conduct by Defendants, as set forth herein. At all times material hereto, Plaintiff was working out

of Defendants’ Fort Washington address.


II. Operative Facts.

       10.     On or about May 1, 2016, Defendants hired Plaintiff as a team member.

       11.     Beginning in or around February of 2021, Plaintiff became the target of severe

and pervasive sexual harassment by one of Defendants’ employees, Jonathan.

       12.     Jonathan repeatedly touched Plaintiff’s arms and shoulders in a sexual manner,

with Plaintiff each time making it clear to Jonathan that this touching was unwanted.

       13.     On one occasion, Jonathan grabbed Plaintiff from behind, under her breasts.

        Case 2:22-cv-03244-CSMW Document 1 Filed 08/15/22 Page 6 of 13




       14.     On another occasion, Jonathan grabbed Plaintiff by the waist again, this time

while facing Plaintiff, and pressed his lower body against Plaintiff.

       15.     On another occasion, Jonathan grabbed Plaintiff’s breasts.

       16.     Each time, Plaintiff rebuffed Jonathan and told him his behavior was harassment

and was inappropriate.

       17.     Plaintiff made complaints to Defendants regarding Jonathan’s behavior, but was

told that it was an “isolated incident.”

       18.     Jonathan was then moved to second shift; however, Plaintiff was still forced to

interact with Jonathan during her work day.

       19.     Plaintiff additionally witnessed Jonathan aggressively sexually harassing other

female employees, and even female customers of Defendants.

       20.     Plaintiff continued to make complaints with Defendants regarding Jonathan’s

behavior, for approximately six (6) months while Jonathan’s conduct was allowed to continue

unchecked.

       21.     Plaintiff as well as the other female employees of Defendants were subjected to

severe and pervasive sexual harassment and a hostile work environment due to Jonathan, and

Defendants failed in their duty to rectify the situation or otherwise take action to protect their

employees.

       22.     At all times material hereto, Defendants created and maintained a hostile work

environment that Plaintiff was subjected to and required to work in and around.

       23.     At all times material hereto, Defendants fostered an atmosphere of tolerance for

sexual harassment of women, including Plaintiff, within Defendants’ place of business.

III. Causes of Action.

                                     COUNT I
           VIOLATIONS OF TITLE VII OF THE CIVIL RIGHTS ACT OF 1964
                (Hostile Work Environment; 42 U.S.C.A. § 2000 et seq)
