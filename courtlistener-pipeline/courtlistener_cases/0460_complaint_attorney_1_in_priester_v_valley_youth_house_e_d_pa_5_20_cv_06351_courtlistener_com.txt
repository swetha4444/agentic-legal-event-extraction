                                 CIVIL ACTION COMPLAINT

I. Parties and Reasons for Jurisdiction.

       1.       Plaintiff, GREGORY PRIESTER (hereinafter “Plaintiff”) is an adult individual

residing at the above address.

       2.       Defendant, VALLEY YOUTH HOUSE (hereinafter “Defendant”), is a

corporation organized by and operating under the laws of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania

and having a corporate headquarters at the above captioned address.

       3.       At all times material hereto, Defendant qualifies as Plaintiff’s employers pursuant

to the Title VII of the Civil Rights Act and the Pennsylvania Human Relations Act (“PHRA”).

       4.       Plaintiff has exhausted his administrative remedies pursuant to the Equal

Employment Opportunity Act and the Pennsylvania Human Relations Act. (See Exhibit A, a

true and correct copy of a “right-to-sue” letter issued by the Equal Employment Opportunity

Commission.)

       5.       This action is instituted pursuant to the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the

Pennsylvania Human Relations Act and applicable federal law.
            Case 5:20-cv-06351-JMG Document 1 Filed 12/17/20 Page 5 of 15




       6.       Jurisdiction is conferred by 28 U.S.C. §§ 1331 and 1343.

       7.       Supplemental jurisdiction over the Plaintiff’s state law claims is conferred

pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1367.

       8.       Pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1391(b)(1) and (b)(2), venue is properly laid in this

district because Defendant conducts business in this district, and because a substantial part of the

acts and/or omissions giving rise to the claims set forth herein occurred in this judicial district.

Plaintiff was working in the Eastern District of Pennsylvania at the time of the illegal actions by

Defendant as set forth herein.

II. Operative Facts.

       8.       Plaintiff is an African American male.

       9.       On or about January 21, 2019, Plaintiff was hired by Defendant as a maintenance

worker at Defendant’s Achieving Independence Center located at 1415 N. Broad Street, Suite

100, Philadelphia, PA 19122.

       10.      At all times material hereto, Plaintiff was the only African-American employee in

his department, with all of his co-workers being Caucasian.

       11.      Beginning in June of 2019, Plaintiff began to be subjected to racial harassment on

behalf of Defendant’s supervisor, Fran Haar, a Caucasian man.

       12.      Mr. Haar stated to Plaintiff, “All you niggers are hard-headed.”

       13.      Plaintiff made a complaint to Defendant’s regional supervisor Gus Warren, a

Caucasian man, regarding the harassment.

       14.      Mr. White stated he would “talk to Mr. Haar”; however, Mr. Haar’s behavior

toward Plaintiff did not change.
           Case 5:20-cv-06351-JMG Document 1 Filed 12/17/20 Page 6 of 15




         15.     Mr. Haar continued to speak abusively toward Plaintiff and use racial slurs,

including the “n word.”

         16.     Plaintiff lodged several more complaints with HR regarding Mr. Haar but nothing

was done to address Mr. Haar’s conduct.

         17.     On or about September 26, 2019, Mr. Haar confronted Plaintiff regarding a work

issue.

         18.     During this encounter, Mr. Haar again said to Plaintiff, “All you niggers are hard-

headed.”

         19.     Plaintiff told Mr. Haar not to say that to him again.

         20.     Mr. Haar repeated the statement, and also stated “You niggers never listen”, and

Plaintiff left the office.

         21.     Later that day, Plaintiff was summoned to a meeting with Defendant’s Human

Resources department.

         22.     Plaintiff spoke with Defendant’s Human Resources representative, Sherice, who

indicated that Mr. Haar stated that Plaintiff had “threatened” him.

         23.     Plaintiff explained what had happened and what Mr. Haar said, but was sent

home.

         24.     On September 27, 2019, Plaintiff was informed his employment was being

terminated for “threatening” his supervisor.

         25.     Defendant’s explanation to Plaintiff concerning the reason for his termination was

untrue pretext and discriminatory retaliation towards Plaintiff for lodging his race discrimination

complaints against Mr. Haar.
           Case 5:20-cv-06351-JMG Document 1 Filed 12/17/20 Page 7 of 15




         26.   Mr. Haar’s behavior was never addressed even after Plaintiff made several

complaints regarding the harassing and discriminatory manner in which he was being treated.

III. Causes of Action.

                      COUNT I – EMPLOYMENT DISCRIMINATION
                                (42 U.S.C.A. § 2000e-2(a))

         27.   Plaintiff incorporates paragraphs 1-26 as if fully set forth at length herein.

         28.   Defendant took adverse action against Plaintiff by terminating his employment.

         29.   Plaintiff’s race was a motivating factor in Defendant’s decision to terminate his

employment.

         30.   Plaintiff was subjected to a hostile work environment by Defendant, as set forth

above.

         31.   As such, Defendant’s decision to terminate Plaintiff’s employment is an unlawful

employment practice, under 42. U.S.C. § 2000e-2(a).
